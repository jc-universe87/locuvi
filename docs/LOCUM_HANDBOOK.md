## 1. Before your first session: the checklist

You can't start working until a few things are in place. Most take weeks, not days, so start
early, ideally before you finish training.

| ✅ | What | Why it matters | Tips |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ☐ | **GMC registration with a licence to practise**, and on the **GP Register** | You can't work as a GP without it | Keep your GMC Online details (especially your email address) up to date. Other services use them |
| ☐ | **Performers List** | You must be on it to do NHS GP work | In England, the National Performers List, run through PCSE Online. If you were a GP registrar, change your status to "GP Performer". Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own lists |
| ☐ | **Enhanced DBS check** | Needed for the Performers List | Often done as part of joining the list. Keep a copy |
| ☐ | **Indemnity** | Protects you if something goes wrong | See below: in England most NHS clinical work is covered by a state scheme, but you still need your own medical defence membership |
| ☐ | **Register as self-employed with HMRC** | You'll pay your own tax | Register for Self Assessment by **5 October** after the end of the tax year in which you started (see [Tax and money](#9-tax-and-money)) |
| ☐ | **A bank account for your locum income** | Keeps business money separate | Not a legal requirement for a sole trader, but it makes your records, tax and Locuvi's bank matching much simpler |
| ☐ | **An accountant who knows GPs** | Pension and tax for locums have quirks | Ask colleagues or your LMC for recommendations |
| ☐ | **Decide about the NHS pension** | Locum work can count towards it | See [Your NHS pension](#8-your-nhs-pension). You'll need your SD number (your NHS pension scheme number) |
| ☐ | **NHSmail and a smartcard** | Many practices need them for their systems | Once you're on the Performers List you can ask for a locum NHSmail account |
| ☐ | **Support networks** | You won't have a practice team behind you | The BMA (sessional GPs), NASGP, your Local Medical Committee (LMC) and local locum groups are all worth joining |

### Indemnity in plain English

- **England:** since April 2019 the **Clinical Negligence Scheme for General Practice
  (CNSGP)**, run by NHS Resolution, automatically covers clinical negligence claims for NHS
  work, including locum work. It does **not** cover private work, GMC investigations,
  inquests, complaints support or medico-legal advice. You still need membership of a
  **medical defence organisation (MDO)** for those. Tell your MDO you work as a locum.
- **Wales:** the state scheme (GMPI) covers locums on the **All Wales Locum Register**, so
  join it if you work there. You still need an MDO for the things the scheme excludes.
- **Scotland and Northern Ireland:** different arrangements. Ask your MDO what you need.

> [!TIP]
> Keep a folder (paper or digital) with your GMC certificate, Performers List confirmation,
> DBS, indemnity certificate, CV, references, hepatitis B immunity and training certificates.
> Practices often ask for copies before your first session. Having it ready makes you easy to
> book.

## 2. Setting up Locuvi (about ten minutes)

Locuvi asks three things when you first open it. The [user manual](USER_GUIDE.md#3-your-first-five-minutes)
has pictures for each step.

1. **Your details.** Your name, address, email and phone go on your invoices.
2. **Bank account.** The details practices use to pay you. They're printed on your invoices
   and shown masked (••••1234) on screen.
3. **Your first practice.** Name, billing address, the **Email address for invoices** and
   your **Usual fee**.

Then, when you have a moment:

- **Settings › NHS pension:** add your **contribution rate (tier)** so Locuvi can work out
  your Form B total, plus your **NHS pension reference (SD number)** and **GMC number** for
  the pension worksheet.
- **Settings › Invoice settings:** choose **Days before an invoice is due**. 14 or 30 days are
  common; use whatever you've agreed with practices.
- **Settings › Invoice appearance:** add your logo, a colour and a footer line, such as
  "Payment within 14 days, please quote the invoice number".
- **Settings › Email invoices:** send invoices from your own email address
  ([how](EMAIL_SETUP.md#part-2-sending-invoices-from-your-email-address)).
- **Settings › Phone or computer calendar:** see your bookings in your phone's calendar
  ([how](USER_GUIDE.md#14-settings-one-by-one)).

> [!TIP]
> Add every practice's **Email address for invoices** even if you post invoices. Locuvi also
> uses it to recognise that practice's booking emails.

## 3. Finding work and agreeing terms

### Where work comes from

- **Practices directly.** Most locum work comes from practice managers who know you. Doing a
  good job and being easy to book (quick replies, clear terms, tidy invoices) brings repeat
  work.
- **Local locum groups, LMC lists and locum chambers.** Good for getting started in a new
  area.
- **Agencies.** These can fill gaps, but see the pension warning in
  [section 8](#8-your-nhs-pension): work arranged and paid through an agency usually can't be
  pensioned as freelance locum work.

### Agreeing the fee

Before you accept a booking, be clear about:

- **What a "session" means.** The start and finish times, how many appointments and how long
  each is, whether home visits, telephone lists, prescriptions, results or letters are
  included, and whether you're expected to be the duty doctor.
- **Session or hourly.** Locuvi handles both. In each practice's settings choose
  **How is the fee agreed?** › **Per session** or **Per hour**.
- **Overruns.** What happens if the session runs late? Many locums charge for extra time in
  set blocks.
- **Pension contribution.** If you pension your work, the practice pays an NHS employer
  contribution on top of your fee. Agree whether your figure is **before** or **already
  including** it. Locuvi has a switch for this: **Practice's NHS pension contribution** ›
  **Added to agreed fee** or **Already in agreed fee**. Locuvi then works the numbers out
  correctly either way.

> [!TIP]
> **Example (made-up figures).** Fee £600 a session, pension contribution added on top. 90% of
> the fee is pensionable (£540). The practice adds 14.38% of that (£77.65), so the practice
> pays £677.65. Locuvi does this sum on every invoice, using the rate for the date of the work.
> If a practice says "£677.65 including pension", choose **Already in agreed fee** and Locuvi
> works backwards to the £600 basic fee.

There are no official national locum rates. Fees vary by area, by day of the week and by
how short the notice is. Ask local locums, your LMC or locum groups what's usual.

### Put your terms in writing

A short set of written terms, sent with your first booking at each practice, prevents most
arguments. The BMA and NASGP publish model terms and locum agreements you can adapt. Cover:

- your fee, and what a session includes,
- overrun charges,
- **cancellation**: for example, you'll charge in full if the practice cancels at short
  notice,
- **payment terms**: how many days the practice has to pay,
- **pension**: that the practice will approve your Form A promptly on PCSE Online,
- how you'll invoice (monthly or per booking) and who to send invoices to.

In Locuvi, store the practice-specific parts in the practice's settings:
- **Days allowed to pay**, if it differs from your usual payment deadline.
- **How this practice wants invoices**, for example "send to accounts@…, quote the rota
  reference".
- **Practice requires a PO number**, if they use purchase orders.
- **Person who signs Form A**.

## 4. Bookings and your diary

**Confirm every booking in writing** with the date, session and fee. An email is enough.
It's your evidence if anything is disputed later.

Ways to get bookings into Locuvi:

| How | Best for | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Tap the days** | A few sessions | **Diary**: choose the practice and the type of work, then tap the days |
| **Repeating bookings** | Regular weekly work | **Diary** › repeat ([how](USER_GUIDE.md#regular-work)) |
| **Paste a booking email** | A practice sends a list of dates | **Add bookings from a message**: Locuvi finds the dates, and you check and accept them |
| **Let Locuvi read your email** | Lots of booking emails | **Settings › Booking emails** ([how](EMAIL_SETUP.md)) |

Locuvi warns you about:
- the same session entered twice,
- two bookings that overlap,
- a fee that's different from usual,
- a cancellation in a booking email.

It never adds anything to your diary until you've checked it.

> [!TIP]
> Make an email folder (or Gmail label) called **Locum** with a rule that files booking emails
> into it, and point Locuvi at that folder only. Locuvi then reads only those emails and
> ignores everything else.

**Cancellations.** If a practice cancels, remove the session from your diary. If the
cancellation arrives by email, Locuvi spots it and offers **Cancel this session**. If your
terms include a cancellation fee, keep the session and change its fee to the cancellation
fee, so it's invoiced. Keep the practice's cancellation email in your Inbox as a record.

**See your bookings everywhere.** Subscribe to your Locuvi calendar from your phone
(**Settings › Phone or computer calendar**). New and cancelled bookings then show up by
themselves. **Download a copy** gives you a calendar file or a spreadsheet of all your
sessions, which is handy for appraisal.

## 5. On the day

Every practice works differently, and you'll be working without the background knowledge the
regular team has. Good locums ask early.

**Before you start your list, ask for:**
- **Logins** for the clinical system (often EMIS Web or SystmOne), your smartcard working,
  and printers, prescription paper and the e-referral system set up.
- **The locum pack**, if the practice has one: key phone numbers, referral pathways,
  safeguarding leads, the on-call or duty doctor, test result handling, local formulary.
- **Who to ask** if you're stuck, and how to call for help in an emergency (alarm button,
  emergency drugs and defibrillator location).
- **What's expected at the end:** tasks, results, letters, home visits, and who picks up
  anything you can't finish.

**During and after:**
- Keep to the session you agreed. If you're asked to do more, agree it (and the fee) at the
  time.
- Write clear, complete notes. Another clinician may pick up where you left off.
- Before you leave, hand over anything outstanding, in writing.
- Note any extra time worked the same day. In Locuvi, add it as **Hours**, or adjust the
  session.

> [!TIP]
> Keep a private learning log for your appraisal: interesting cases, things you looked up,
> significant events. **Never put patient-identifiable details in it**, and never put patient
> information in Locuvi. Locuvi is for your business admin only.

## 6. Invoicing

**Invoice promptly.** The sooner you invoice, the sooner you're paid, and your pension
deadlines depend on it (see [section 8](#8-your-nhs-pension)). Most locums invoice monthly,
straight after the month ends. Some invoice after each booking.

Locuvi makes this one tap:

1. When a month ends, **Today** says *"You can invoice for September"*.
2. Tap **Create invoice**. The draft fills itself in: dates, fees and the practice's pension
   contribution.
3. Check it, then tap **Issue invoice and download**. It gets its number and a PDF.
4. Tap **Send** to email it. Nothing is ever sent without your tap.

What Locuvi gets right for you:

- **Numbered invoices, in order.** Each issued invoice has a unique number and can't be
  edited afterwards. This is what accountants and HMRC expect.
- **Mistakes are fixed with a credit note,** not by editing. If you overcharged, issue a
  **credit note** (it has its own number, starting CN-), then a new invoice if needed. See
  [Corrections](USER_GUIDE.md#9-corrections-cancelling-and-credit-notes).
- **NHS work across two months** is split into separate invoices, because pension forms are
  monthly.
- **PO numbers.** Locuvi reminds you if a practice needs one.
- **No VAT.** Medical care by a registered doctor is generally exempt from VAT, so locum
  invoices don't normally include it. Locuvi doesn't create VAT invoices. Ask your accountant
  if you do other kinds of work.

> [!TIP]
> Practices pay faster when the invoice is easy to process. That means the right address, a
> PO number if they use them, your bank details, the dates and sessions listed, and the
> pension contribution shown separately. Locuvi includes all of these.

## 7. Getting paid, and what to do when you aren't

**Record payments** when they arrive:
- tap **Record as paid** on the invoice, or
- upload your bank statement in **Invoices › Check bank payments**. Locuvi suggests which
  payment is for which invoice, and keeps only money coming in. Your spending is never
  stored.

**When a payment is late**, Locuvi shows it on **Today** with the number of days late. A
calm routine works best:

1. **On the due date:** check the practice received the invoice. Invoices get lost in shared
   inboxes.
2. **A week late:** a short, friendly reminder quoting the invoice number, the amount and the
   due date.
3. **Two to three weeks late:** phone the practice manager.
4. **Still unpaid:** a formal letter. Businesses in the UK have a legal right to charge
   **statutory interest** on late payments between businesses (8% above the Bank of England
   base rate), plus fixed compensation of **£40, £70 or £100** depending on the size of the
   debt. Mentioning this usually gets the invoice paid. See
   [late commercial payments](https://www.gov.uk/late-commercial-payments-interest-debt-recovery).

> [!TIP]
> Practices with a slow payment record get a shorter leash next time: invoice the day the
> work ends, or set fewer **Days allowed to pay** in that practice's settings.

## 8. Your NHS pension

As a freelance GP locum in England you can choose to add your NHS locum work to the NHS
Pension Scheme. Many locums find it worth doing, because the practice pays an employer
contribution on top of your fee. Whether it's right for you depends on your circumstances,
so talk to an accountant or financial adviser who knows the NHS scheme.

**Locuvi has a step-by-step guide built in:** open **Pension** and tap
**How to send your pension forms**. In short:

| Form | What | When |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Form A** ("Locum A") | One for each practice: the work you did and the fee. The practice approves it on PCSE Online | Send soon after the work. The work can only be pensioned if the form arrives **within 10 weeks** of the work ending |
| **Form B** ("Locum B") | Once a month: the pensionable fees **you were paid** that month | By the **7th of the following month** |
| **Payment** | Your contribution plus the practice's contribution | By the **7th of the following month**, usually by card on PCSE Online |

**Which work can be pensioned as freelance locum work:**
- ✅ Work you do **as an individual**, self-employed, invoiced in your own name, covering or
  helping a practice temporarily.
- ❌ Work invoiced through **your own limited company**.
- ❌ Work arranged and paid through an **agency** or a commercial deputising service.
- ⚠️ **Long-term regular work at one practice** (for example six months or more) may count
  as salaried work rather than locum work. Ask PCSE.

Only **90%** of your basic fee is pensionable. Locuvi does this sum for you.

**Locuvi's part:**
- works out every figure for Form A and Form B,
- tracks whether each practice has approved its Form A,
- reminds you on **Today** before the 10-week and 7th-of-the-month deadlines,
- lets you compare with your PCSE record later.

You still send the forms and pay, on PCSE Online. Locuvi never submits anything.

> [!WARNING]
> Missing the 10-week deadline means that work can't be added to your pension at all.
> Practices are sometimes slow to approve Form A. Send it early and chase gently; Locuvi
> reminds you to.

Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own forms and addresses; the in-app guide
says where they go.

## 9. Tax and money

As a self-employed locum you are a **sole trader**. Nobody takes tax off your fees, so you
pay it yourself through **Self Assessment**.

### The key dates

| Date | What |
| --- | --- |
| **6 April – 5 April** | The UK tax year |
| **5 October** | Register for Self Assessment, if this is your first year as self-employed (for the tax year that ended the previous April) |
| **31 January** | Online tax return due, and tax due: the balance for last year, plus your first "payment on account" for this year |
| **31 July** | Second "payment on account" |

### Watch out for the first-year tax bill

**Payments on account** are advance payments towards next year's tax, each normally half of
last year's bill. In your first year this can mean that on 31 January you pay a whole year's
tax **plus** half as much again in advance. Many new locums are caught out by this.

> [!TIP]
> Open a separate savings account and move a fixed share of **every payment** into it the day
> it arrives. Ask your accountant what share suits your income. Locuvi's **Reports** show
> what you've received this tax year, so you can check you've put enough aside.

### Income, expenses and records

- **Income is usually counted when you're paid** ("cash basis"). That has been the default
  for sole traders since the 2024–25 tax year. In **Reports**, choose **Show income by** ›
  **Date you were paid** to match. Your accountant may prefer **Date you invoiced**.
- **Keep the practices' pension contributions separate** from your fees. They pass through to
  the pension scheme. Locuvi's **Figures for your tax return** keeps them apart.
- **Allowable expenses** reduce your tax. For locums these commonly include:
  - medical defence membership
  - GMC and professional subscriptions on HMRC's approved list (for example the BMA)
  - medical equipment
  - travel to temporary workplaces
  - accountancy fees
  - a share of home costs if you do your admin at home

  Check with your accountant, and keep every receipt. Locuvi doesn't track expenses, so keep
  them in a spreadsheet or your accountant's app.
- **National Insurance:** self-employed people pay Class 4 National Insurance through Self
  Assessment. Class 2 is no longer required (since April 2024).
- **Keep your records** for at least 5 years after the 31 January deadline for each tax year.
  Locuvi keeps every invoice and payment, and **Reports › All documents (ZIP)** gives your
  accountant every PDF for the year.

### Making Tax Digital for Income Tax (MTD)

HMRC is moving sole traders to **quarterly digital updates**:

- **From April 2026:** if your self-employment (plus any property) income was over **£50,000**
  in the 2024–25 tax year.
- **From April 2027:** over **£30,000**.

If you're in scope, you need MTD-compatible software and you send HMRC four quarterly updates
plus a final return. **Locuvi isn't MTD software**, but its **Invoice list (CSV)** and
**Download payments (CSV)** give you or your accountant the income figures to put into MTD
software. See [Use Making Tax Digital for Income Tax](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/use-making-tax-digital-for-income-tax).

### No sick pay, no holiday pay

Nobody pays you when you're ill or on holiday. Many locums budget for around 6 to 8 weeks
off a year and consider **income protection insurance**. Get independent advice.

## 10. Appraisal, revalidation and CPD

You still need a yearly **appraisal** and to **revalidate** with the GMC every five years.
As a locum you have to collect the evidence yourself:

- **A record of your work:** which practices, how many sessions, what kind of work. In
  Locuvi, use **Settings › Phone or computer calendar › Spreadsheet (.csv)** for a list of
  every session, or **Reports › By practice** for totals.
- **CPD and reflections:** keep notes as you go. It's much harder to remember at appraisal
  time.
- **Quality improvement and significant events:** ask the practices you work at regularly if
  you can take part in their audits and significant event meetings.
- **Colleague and patient feedback:** needed once per revalidation cycle. Ask practices you
  work at often to help you collect it.

## 11. Looking after yourself

Locum work gives you freedom, but it can be isolating.

- **Don't overbook.** It's tempting to say yes to everything early on. Leave time for admin,
  CPD and rest.
- **Stay connected:** locum groups, your LMC, BMA sessional GP events, peer learning groups.
- **Support is there:** **NHS Practitioner Health** is a free, confidential service for
  doctors with mental health concerns. Your MDO and the BMA also run support lines.
- **Do admin little and often.** Ten minutes a week in Locuvi beats a lost weekend before the
  tax deadline.

## 14. Words you'll hear

| Word | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Locum** | A doctor covering or helping a practice temporarily |
| **Session** | A block of work, usually a morning (AM) or afternoon (PM) surgery |
| **LMC** | Local Medical Committee: represents GPs in an area, often keeps lists of locums |
| **MDO** | Medical defence organisation: provides indemnity, advice and support |
| **CNSGP** | The state-backed clinical negligence scheme for NHS general practice in England |
| **PCSE** | Primary Care Support England: runs the Performers List and GP pensions for NHS England |
| **SD number** | Your NHS Pension Scheme membership number |
| **Tier** | Your own NHS pension contribution rate, based on your pensionable pay |
| **Form A / Form B** | The monthly NHS pension forms for freelance locums (see [section 8](#8-your-nhs-pension)) |
| **Sole trader** | Self-employed in your own name, not through a company |
| **Self Assessment** | The way self-employed people tell HMRC about their income and pay tax |
| **Payment on account** | An advance payment towards next year's tax, due 31 January and 31 July |
| **MTD** | Making Tax Digital: quarterly digital tax updates for higher-earning sole traders |
| **PO number** | Purchase order number: a reference some practices need on every invoice |
| **Credit note** | A document that cancels all or part of an issued invoice |

For words used in the app itself, see the user manual's [Words explained](USER_GUIDE.md#19-words-explained).
